Direct target ≠ downstream effect

Alpelisib directly inhibits PI3Kα. A later change in AKT signaling or protein abundance does not make it an AKT, ALK, or N-Myc-binding drug.

MYCN amplification ≠ one drug match

Extra MYCN DNA copies can support an oncogenic program. None of the drugs here directly deletes those copies. Related vulnerabilities need their own evidence.

Separate drugs ≠ a safe combination

Blocking two nodes does not prove synergy. Overlapping toxicity and drug interactions can outweigh a plausible pathway diagram.

How to read it

The horizontal labels are cancer categories. Each stacked bar shows a reported alteration frequency; the colors distinguish alteration classes. “CNA” means copy-number alteration. The + rows refer to data availability, not positive drug responses.

  • Green: mutation
  • Purple: structural variant
  • Red: amplification
  • Blue: deep deletion
  • Gray: multiple alterations

What the colors do not establish

Amplification means extra copies; it is not the same as an activating point mutation or a fusion. A deletion may remove a gene rather than create an inhibitor target. Different changes in the same gene can have different—or unknown—functional effects.

Before connecting it to a drug

Recover the gene and original query, cohort denominators, and profiling definitions. Then establish the specific alteration’s function and the evidence for the exact drug in the exact tumor type. A higher bar is not a higher chance of response, and these are not an individual tumor’s variant allele fractions.
